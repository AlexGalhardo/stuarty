## You will learn

- how to place audio on an audio track;
- how to set volume, fades and mute;
- how to lower music under speech (<Term id="ducking">ducking</Term>) and mark the beats of a song;
- how the export mixes the audio and what the lint warns about.

## The idea

Sound comes from two places: the audio of a video clip, and **audio items** on an audio track. An audio item points to an audio asset, or to the audio of a video asset, and plays from `source_in` for a duration. The export mixes every audible item into one 48 kHz stereo track.

Each clip or audio item has a `volume` (linear gain from 0 to 4), a `fade_in`, a `fade_out`, and a clip can be muted with `audio_muted`. Volume can also be animated with a [keyframe](/docs/guide/keyframes) on the property `volume`. The project has a `loudness_target` in <Term id="lufs">LUFS</Term> (from -30 to -5) that the export aims for.

In the editor, the inspector of a clip or an audio item has an **Audio** section with the volume, the two fades and **Mute audio**. The CLI, an agent and the command palette (`Ctrl+K`, `Cmd+K` on macOS) reach the same operations.

The default duration of an audio item is the rest of the asset from `source_in`. The two fades together cannot be longer than the item. A volume of 1, fades of `0s` and `audio_muted` false clear the stored value.

## Duck music under speech

`audio.duck` lowers a music item while speech sounds. It writes [volume keyframes](/docs/guide/keyframes) on the music: the volume drops by `gain_db` (-12 by default, from -40 to -1) over `attack` (0.2s) before each stretch of speech and comes back over `release` (0.5s) after it. Two stretches of speech closer than the ramps are held low across the gap. `speech_item_ids` names the clips or audio items that carry the speech (1 to 20).

The speech is found by the analysis of those items, which the service reads for you; never send `resolved`. The music item must have no volume keyframes yet. To undo the ducking, clear them with `keyframe.clear` on `volume`.

```bash
stuarty edit --project prj_7k2m9x4q1b audio.duck item_id=itm_s6t7v8w9x0   speech_item_ids:=[itm_m1n2p3q4r5] gain_db:=-14
```

If the speech item has no analysis yet the edit fails with `E_INVALID_STATE` and the hint to wait until the asset is ready.

## Mark the beats of a song

`audio.mark_beats` writes a marker on a music item at each <Term id="beat">beat</Term> of its visible part, so you can place cuts and transitions on the music. `every` marks every nth beat (1 to 64, default 1), `label` names the markers (up to 60 characters) and `replace` (true by default) replaces the markers the item already has. The beat grid comes from the analysis of the asset. Markers change neither the picture nor the sound.

```bash
stuarty edit --project prj_7k2m9x4q1b audio.mark_beats item_id=itm_s6t7v8w9x0 every:=2 label=beat
```

An asset without audio has no beat grid and the edit fails with `E_INVALID_STATE`. The same two operations are available to an MCP agent through `edit`. In the editor, the inspector of a music item has a **Ducking** section (the speech items, the volume change, attack and release) and a **Beat markers** section (every nth beat, a label, replace the markers of the item).

Run `stuarty lint` before you export. These rules concern sound: `L_AUDIO_OVERLAP` (audio items that overlap), `L_AUDIO_CLIP` (the mix reaches clipping) and `L_SILENT_EXPORT` (the export would have no sound).

## Common mistakes

- Adding audio to a visual track: `E_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
- Setting `audio_muted` on an audio item. It applies to clips; set `volume` to 0 for an audio item.
- A volume above 4: `E_VALIDATION`.
- Ducking a music item that already has volume keyframes: the edit is refused. Clear them first.
