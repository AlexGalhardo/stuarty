## The idea

Editing has a lot of strange words: *jump cut*, *codec*, *chroma key*. This glossary explains them in plain words, as if you were ten years old and wanted to start a career in video. Every term has:

1. a short **meaning** in everyday words;
2. an **example** you can picture;
3. when it matters, an **In Stuarty** line that says where to find it in the editor or which command does it;
4. **See also** links to the words next to it;
5. **Learn more** links to a trusted source: the standards bodies (ITU, EBU, SMPTE, W3C), the FFmpeg and MDN documentation, the Library of Congress and encyclopedia articles.

The words are grouped by topic, in the order of the work: you shoot, edit, cut, mix the sound, grade the colour, add words, add effects, pick a format and deliver.

## Quick start

In the other pages of this site, some words have a dotted underline, such as <Term id="keyframe">keyframe</Term>. Hover over one, or move to it with the keyboard, and a short explanation appears. Click it to read the full entry.

Not sure where to begin? Read these five first: <Term id="clip">clip</Term>, <Term id="timeline">timeline</Term>, <Term id="cut">cut</Term>, <Term id="frame-rate">frame rate</Term> and <Term id="codec">codec</Term>.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the editor changes your original files. It does not: a clip only points at a part of an asset.
- Mixing up a *codec* and a *container*. The container is the box (like MP4); the codec is how what is inside is squeezed (like H.264).
- Mixing up *captions* and *subtitles*. Captions also describe sounds; subtitles usually only write the speech.
