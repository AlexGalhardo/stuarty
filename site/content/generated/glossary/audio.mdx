Terms on this page: 36. Half of a video is what you hear. These are the words for sound.

### Ambience

Ambience tells the viewer where they are. A forest has birds, a street has cars.

**Example.** Adding the sound of waves to a beach video recorded in a quiet room.

**Also called:** atmosphere, background sound. **See also:** [Room tone](#room-tone), [Sound effect](#sound-effect).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Ambience (sound recording)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambience_(sound_recording))

### Audio

Audio is everything you hear. People forgive a slightly bad picture, but not bad sound, so audio matters a lot.

**Example.** A great shot with a crackling voice is hard to watch.

**In Stuarty.** Sound lives on audio tracks. See [Audio](/docs/guide/audio).

**See also:** [Dialogue](#dialogue), [Mix](#mix).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound)

### Audio pan

Panning places a sound between the left and the right speaker, so it seems to come from a place.

**Example.** A bird chirping from the left side of the picture.

**See also:** [Stereo](#stereo).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Panning (audio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panning_(audio))

### Beat

The beat is the regular pulse in a song. Cutting your video on the beat makes it feel good.

**Example.** Clap along to a song: each clap is a beat.

**In Stuarty.** `audio.mark_beats` writes a marker on each beat. See [Audio](/docs/guide/audio).

**See also:** [BPM](#bpm), [Rhythm](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#rhythm), [Marker](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#marker).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Beat (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music))

### Bit depth

More bits mean finer steps between quiet and loud, and less noise in quiet parts. 16 bits is common, 24 bits is used for recording.

**Example.** A 24-bit recording keeps the quiet details better than 8 bits.

**See also:** [Sample rate](#sample-rate).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Audio bit depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_bit_depth)

### BPM

BPM counts the beats in one minute. 60 BPM is one beat each second. 120 BPM is two beats each second.

**Example.** A slow ballad may be 70 BPM and a dance track 128 BPM.

**Also called:** tempo. **See also:** [Beat](#beat).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo)

### Clipping

When a sound goes above the highest level, the top of the wave is chopped flat and it crackles. Once it is clipped you cannot fix it, so avoid it when recording.

**Example.** A shout into a phone microphone sounds ugly and broken.

**See also:** [Peak](#peak), [Headroom](#headroom).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Clipping (audio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipping_(audio))

### Compressor

A compressor squeezes the difference between loud and quiet. Voices become easier to understand, because the whispers and the shouts are closer together.

**Example.** A narrator who whispers and then shouts sounds steady after compression.

**Also called:** dynamic range compression. **See also:** [Limiter](#limiter), [Normalization](#normalization).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Dynamic range compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_range_compression)

### Decibel

Decibels measure loudness on a scale that matches how ears hear: a small number change can be a big difference in how loud it seems. Adding 6 dB roughly doubles the strength of a signal.

**Example.** A whisper is about 30 dB and a rock concert about 110 dB.

**Also called:** dB. **See also:** [Volume](#volume), [Peak](#peak).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Decibel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel)

### Dialogue

Dialogue is spoken conversation. It carries the story, so it needs to be clear above everything else.

**Example.** Two friends talking in a scene.

**See also:** [Voice-over](#voice-over), [Ducking](#ducking).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue)

### Ducking

Ducking lowers the music when a voice speaks and lets it come back after, so you can always hear the words.

**Example.** Radio presenters do it: the song gets quieter when they speak.

**In Stuarty.** `audio.duck` lowers a music item under speech. See [Audio](/docs/guide/audio).

**Also called:** sidechain. **See also:** [Music bed](#music-bed), [Dialogue](#dialogue).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Ducking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducking)

### Equalizer

An equalizer works like the bass and treble knobs on a speaker. Cut the low rumble from a voice or boost the highs to make it clearer.

**Example.** Remove the wind rumble from an outdoor interview.

**Also called:** EQ. **See also:** [Noise reduction](#noise-reduction).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Equalization (audio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equalization_(audio))

### Foley

Foley artists make small sounds by hand, like footsteps, rustling clothes or cracking celery for breaking bones.

**Example.** Walking on gravel in a studio to make the sound of someone walking in a movie.

**See also:** [Sound effect](#sound-effect).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Foley (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foley_(filmmaking))

### Headroom

Headroom is how much louder the sound could get before it breaks. Leaving some prevents clipping.

**Example.** Recording a voice at a middle level leaves room if the person suddenly laughs loudly.

**See also:** [Clipping](#clipping), [Peak](#peak).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Headroom (audio signal processing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headroom_(audio_signal_processing))

### Limiter

A limiter is a very strict compressor: the sound simply cannot pass the limit, so it cannot clip.

**Example.** A safety net placed at the end of the sound chain.

**See also:** [Compressor](#compressor), [Clipping](#clipping).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Limiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limiter)

### Lip sync

When the picture and the sound are even slightly out of time, a talking person looks wrong. Fixing that is called sync.

**Example.** You hear "hello" a moment after you see the lips close.

**Also called:** A/V sync. **See also:** [Frame accuracy](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#frame-accuracy).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Audio-to-video synchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization)

### Loudness

A short loud bang and a long steady hum can have the same highest point, but the hum feels louder. Loudness measures how loud it feels.

**Example.** Two songs that peak the same can still be one louder than the other.

**In Stuarty.** The project has a `loudness_target` (LUFS) that the export aims for.

**See also:** [LUFS](#lufs), [Peak](#peak).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Loudness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudness) · [ITU-R Recommendation BS.1770 (measuring programme loudness)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770)

### LUFS

LUFS measures loudness the way ears do, averaged over the whole video. Websites and TV have a target number and make loud videos quieter.

**Example.** A platform that wants about -14 LUFS turns a -8 LUFS video down.

**Also called:** LKFS. **See also:** [Loudness](#loudness), [Normalization](#normalization).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: LKFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LKFS) · [ITU-R Recommendation BS.1770 (measuring programme loudness)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770) · [EBU R 128: loudness normalisation and permitted maximum level](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128.pdf)

### Mix

Mixing sets the loudness of voice, music and effects relative to each other, so nothing hides something else.

**Example.** Voice in front, music behind, a small whoosh on top.

**In Stuarty.** An export mixes every audio track together.

**See also:** [Stem](#stem), [Ducking](#ducking).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Audio mixing (recorded music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_mixing_(recorded_music))

### Mono

Mono sound is a single channel. It does not have left and right. Many microphones record mono.

**Example.** A voice recorded with one microphone.

**See also:** [Stereo](#stereo).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Monaural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaural)

### Music bed

A music bed supports a scene without taking attention from the voice. It is usually kept much lower than the speaker.

**Example.** Soft piano under a story told by a narrator.

**See also:** [Ducking](#ducking), [Score](#score).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Background music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_music)

### Noise reduction

Software can learn what the noise sounds like and take it out. Too much makes voices sound robotic.

**Example.** Taking the fridge hum out of a kitchen interview.

**See also:** [Room tone](#room-tone), [Equalizer](#equalizer).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Noise reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_reduction)

### Normalization

Normalizing changes the volume of the whole file by one amount, so the loudest point or the average loudness hits a target.

**Example.** Make a very quiet voice recording as loud as the others.

**See also:** [LUFS](#lufs), [Compressor](#compressor).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Audio normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_normalization)

### Peak

The peak is the loudest instant. If it is too high, the sound distorts.

**Example.** A balloon pop is a tall, short peak.

**See also:** [Clipping](#clipping), [Headroom](#headroom).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Peak programme meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_programme_meter)

### Pitch

A mouse squeak is high-pitched and a lion roar is low-pitched. Changing the pitch makes a voice higher or lower without changing the speed.

**Example.** A chipmunk voice is a voice with the pitch raised.

**See also:** [Equalizer](#equalizer).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Pitch (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_(music))

### Reverb

Reverb is many little echoes that melt together. A bathroom and a big hall each have their own reverb.

**Example.** Clapping in an empty gym: the clap keeps ringing.

**See also:** [Ambience](#ambience).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Reverberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverberation)

### Room tone

Every room has its own faint sound. Recording a bit of it lets editors fill silent gaps so the sound does not suddenly go dead.

**Example.** Filming 30 seconds of the classroom being quiet.

**Also called:** presence, ambience. **See also:** [Ambience](#ambience).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Room tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_tone)

### Sample rate

Digital sound is a list of measurements. The sample rate says how many are taken per second. Video usually uses 48,000 (48 kHz).

**Example.** A music CD uses 44,100 samples per second; video uses 48,000.

**Also called:** kHz. **See also:** [Bit depth](#bit-depth), [Bitrate](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#bitrate).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sampling (signal processing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_(signal_processing))

### Score

A score is music made to match a story, with moods that change with the scenes.

**Example.** The scary music that starts before the monster appears.

**See also:** [Music bed](#music-bed).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score)

### Sound effect

Sound effects make actions feel real or funny. A door slam, a click, a swoosh when a title slides in.

**Example.** A whoosh when a text slides on screen.

**Also called:** SFX. **See also:** [Foley](#foley), [Ambience](#ambience).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sound effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_effect)

### Stem

A stem is one part of the mix saved on its own. Because they are separate, another version, like another language, can be made easily.

**Example.** A file with only the music and another with only the voices.

**See also:** [Mix](#mix).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Stem (audio)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_(audio))

### Stereo

Stereo has a left and a right speaker, which gives a sense of space. A car can drive from the left to the right.

**Example.** Wearing headphones, a bee flies from your left ear to your right.

**In Stuarty.** A WAV export is 16-bit, 48 kHz stereo.

**See also:** [Mono](#mono), [Surround sound](#surround-sound).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Stereophonic sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereophonic_sound)

### Surround sound

Surround adds speakers behind and beside you so sounds can come from all around. 5.1 means five speakers and one for deep bass.

**Example.** A helicopter that seems to fly over your head in a cinema.

**Also called:** 5.1. **See also:** [Stereo](#stereo).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: 5.1 surround sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5.1_surround_sound)

### Voice-over

A voice-over is a narrator talking over the pictures, explaining or telling the story.

**Example.** A nature video where someone tells you about the lion we see.

**Also called:** VO, narration. **See also:** [Dialogue](#dialogue), [Ducking](#ducking).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Voice-over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice-over)

### Volume

Volume is how strong a sound is. Turning it up makes the sound louder, turning it down makes it quieter.

**Example.** Lower the music so you can hear the talking.

**In Stuarty.** An item has a `volume` from 0 to 4 (a multiplier). 1 is the original. See [Audio](/docs/guide/audio).

**Also called:** gain. **See also:** [Decibel](#decibel), [Loudness](#loudness).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Loudness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudness)

### Waveform

A waveform draws the loudness of a sound over time. It lets you see the beats of a song or the silences in a talk.

**Example.** A clap shows as a tall spike on the waveform.

**See also:** [Volume](#volume), [Beat](#beat).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Waveform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveform)
