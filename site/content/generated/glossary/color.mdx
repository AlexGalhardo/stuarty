Terms on this page: 34. Colour decides how a video feels. These words say how it is measured, changed and kept the same from screen to screen.

### Banding

When a gradient has too few colour steps, your eye sees bands instead of a smooth fade. It is more visible after heavy compression.

**Example.** A sunset sky shows rings of orange instead of a smooth glow.

**See also:** [Color depth](#color-depth), [Compression](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#compression).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Colour banding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_banding)

### Brightness

Raising brightness makes every part of the picture lighter, so the dark parts also turn grey. Use it gently.

**Example.** A video filmed in shade made brighter so the face is visible.

**In Vukem Video.** `brightness`, from -1 to 1 (default 0).

**See also:** [Contrast](#contrast), [Exposure](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#exposure).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Brightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightness)

### Chroma subsampling

Eyes see brightness sharper than colour, so videos often store full detail for brightness and a quarter of the detail for colour. This is written 4:2:0.

**Example.** A tiny red text on a green background looks fuzzier in video than in a photo.

**Also called:** 4:2:0, 4:2:2. **See also:** [Luma and chroma](#luma-and-chroma), [Pixel format](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#pixel-format).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Chroma subsampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_subsampling)

### Color correction

Color correction repairs problems: a picture that is too dark, too blue or too yellow. The goal is for white to look white and skin to look like skin.

**Example.** A video filmed indoors looks orange, so you make it a little cooler.

**In Vukem Video.** `clip.set_color` sets brightness, contrast and saturation. See [Effects and color](/docs/guide/effects-and-color).

**See also:** [Color grading](#color-grading), [White balance](#white-balance).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Color correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_correction)

### Color depth

More bits give smoother gradients. With too few, a sky can show ugly steps called banding.

**Example.** A blue sky going from light to dark looks like stripes in 8 bits but smooth in 10 bits.

**Also called:** bit depth, 8-bit, 10-bit. **See also:** [Banding](#banding), [HDR](#hdr).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Color depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_depth)

### Color grading

After the colours are right, grading makes them tell a feeling: warm and golden for a happy memory, cold and blue for a sad one.

**Example.** A movie about the desert is graded orange and yellow.

**See also:** [Color correction](#color-correction), [LUT](#lut), [Look](#look).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Color grading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_grading)

### Color profile

A profile tells the program how to read the colours of an image or video, so they look the same on different devices.

**Example.** A photo that looks the same on your phone and your computer.

**Also called:** ICC profile. **See also:** [Color space](#color-space).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: ICC profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_profile)

### Color space

A colour space is a map between numbers and colours. If a video is saved with one map and played with another, the colours look wrong.

**Example.** A photo that looks dull on one screen and vivid on another may be using a different colour space.

**See also:** [Gamut](#gamut), [Rec. 709](#rec-709), [Color profile](#color-profile).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Color space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_space)

### Color temperature

Low numbers like 2700 K are warm and orange, like a candle. High numbers like 6500 K are cool and blueish, like daylight on a cloudy day.

**Example.** A sunset is warm; a clear sky is cool.

**Also called:** kelvin. **See also:** [White balance](#white-balance).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Color temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_temperature)

### Contrast

A high contrast picture has deep blacks and bright whites. A low contrast one looks flat and grey, like on a foggy day.

**Example.** A foggy video looks better with a bit more contrast.

**In Vukem Video.** `contrast`, from 0 to 2 (default 1).

**See also:** [Brightness](#brightness), [Dynamic range](#dynamic-range).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Contrast (vision)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrast_(vision))

### Dynamic range

High dynamic range means you can see both the dark corner of a room and the bright window at once, instead of one being black or white.

**Example.** A camera with a big dynamic range shows clouds and a face at the same time.

**See also:** [HDR](#hdr), [Contrast](#contrast).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Dynamic range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_range)

### Gamma

Gamma changes how bright the middle of the picture is, while the darkest and the lightest points stay the same.

**Example.** A picture that looks too dark in the middle is helped by raising the gamma.

**See also:** [Contrast](#contrast), [Brightness](#brightness).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Gamma correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_correction) · [ITU-R Recommendation BT.1886 (reference display gamma)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.1886)

### Gamut

A wide gamut can show very strong greens and reds. A small gamut cannot. Colours outside the gamut are squeezed in.

**Example.** A new phone can show richer greens than an old office screen.

**See also:** [Color space](#color-space), [Rec. 2020](#rec-2020).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Gamut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamut)

### Grayscale

Grayscale (black and white) keeps brightness and removes all colour. It is often used for an old-time look.

**Example.** An old photograph.

**In Vukem Video.** The `grayscale` filter of `clip.set_filter`.

**Also called:** black and white. **See also:** [Saturation](#saturation), [Sepia](#sepia).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Grayscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayscale)

### HDR

HDR video can show much brighter highlights and richer colours than usual (SDR) video, on a screen that supports it.

**Example.** Sun glints on water that look really bright on an HDR phone.

**Also called:** high dynamic range. **See also:** [SDR](#sdr), [Rec. 2020](#rec-2020).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: High-dynamic-range video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-dynamic-range_video) · [ITU-R Recommendation BT.2100 (HDR television)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.2100)

### Highlights, midtones and shadows

Editors treat the three parts separately. Highlights are the brightest areas, shadows the darkest and midtones everything in between.

**Example.** Lower the highlights so the sky does not turn pure white.

**See also:** [Contrast](#contrast), [Histogram](#histogram).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Tonality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonality)

### Histogram

A histogram is a little mountain chart. Mountains on the left mean a dark picture, on the right a bright one. It tells you if the picture is too dark or too bright without trusting your eyes.

**Example.** A tall mountain stuck at the right edge means parts are pure white.

**See also:** [Exposure](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#exposure), [Waveform monitor](#waveform-monitor).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Image histogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_histogram)

### Hue

Hue is the name of the colour, apart from how strong or light it is. Moving the hue turns reds into greens, for example.

**Example.** Changing a red car into a blue car by moving its hue.

**See also:** [Saturation](#saturation).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Hue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hue)

### Log footage

Log recording keeps both dark and bright details but looks washed out. You are supposed to add contrast and colour later.

**Example.** A professional camera video looks dull on purpose until it is graded.

**See also:** [Color grading](#color-grading), [LUT](#lut), [Dynamic range](#dynamic-range).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Log profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_profile)

### Look

A look is how colours, contrast and grain are combined to give a particular style, such as "old film" or "clean and bright".

**Example.** A "vintage" look makes your video seem like an old home movie.

**See also:** [Color grading](#color-grading), [LUT](#lut).

### Luma and chroma

Video often keeps the brightness (luma) and the colour (chroma) in separate parts. A black-and-white picture is only luma.

**Example.** A black and white TV shows only luma.

**Also called:** YUV, YCbCr. **See also:** [Chroma subsampling](#chroma-subsampling), [RGB](#rgb).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: YCbCr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YCbCr)

### LUT

A LUT (look-up table) is a recipe: for every input colour, it says which colour to show. Editors use LUTs to apply a look or to convert a flat camera picture to a normal one.

**Example.** Dropping a "warm film" LUT on a clip to give it a movie look.

**In Vukem Video.** Import a .cube file as a lut asset and apply it to a clip with clip.set_color (lut_asset and lut_intensity).

**See also:** [Color grading](#color-grading), [Log footage](#log-footage).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Lookup table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lookup_table)

### Rec. 2020

Rec. 2020 can show many more colours than Rec. 709. It is used for ultra-high-definition video.

**Example.** A 4K HDR film from a streaming service.

**Also called:** BT.2020. **See also:** [Rec. 709](#rec-709), [HDR](#hdr).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Rec. 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rec._2020) · [ITU-R Recommendation BT.2020 (UHDTV)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.2020)

### Rec. 709

Rec. 709 is the long-time standard for HD television and most web video. Its colours are about the same as sRGB.

**Example.** A YouTube HD video is normally Rec. 709.

**Also called:** BT.709. **See also:** [Rec. 2020](#rec-2020), [Color space](#color-space).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Rec. 709](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rec._709) · [ITU-R Recommendation BT.709 (HDTV colour and format)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.709)

### RGB

Screens make colours from tiny red, green and blue lights. Mix them all and you get white, turn them all off and you get black.

**Example.** Red and green lights together make yellow.

**See also:** [Luma and chroma](#luma-and-chroma), [Pixel](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#pixel).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: RGB color model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RGB_color_model)

### Saturation

At full saturation colours are rich and strong. At zero there is no colour at all and the picture is black and white.

**Example.** Turn saturation down and a rainbow fades to grey.

**In Vukem Video.** `saturation`, from 0 to 2 (default 1).

**See also:** [Hue](#hue), [Grayscale](#grayscale).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: HSL and HSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSL_and_HSV)

### SDR

SDR is the normal look of most video for the last decades. It has less bright highlights than HDR.

**Example.** A regular YouTube video is SDR.

**See also:** [HDR](#hdr), [Rec. 709](#rec-709).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Standard-dynamic-range video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-dynamic-range_video)

### Sepia

Sepia colours the picture in brown tones like old photographs.

**Example.** A picture of your grandparents made to look like it is a hundred years old.

**In Vukem Video.** The `sepia` filter of `clip.set_filter`.

**See also:** [Grayscale](#grayscale), [Look](#look).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sepia (color)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepia_(color))

### sRGB

sRGB is the colour map most computers and websites assume. If nobody says otherwise, an image is treated as sRGB.

**Example.** Photos on websites are usually sRGB.

**See also:** [Rec. 709](#rec-709), [Color space](#color-space).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: SRGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRGB)

### Tint

Tint is the other slider next to temperature. It moves the picture between green and magenta, to remove a colour cast.

**Example.** A video under a fluorescent light looks a bit green, so you add some pink.

**See also:** [White balance](#white-balance).

### Vectorscope

A vectorscope is a round chart. The further a dot is from the middle, the stronger its colour. It helps to keep skin tones looking natural.

**Example.** A line on the chart shows where human skin should fall.

**See also:** [Waveform monitor](#waveform-monitor), [Saturation](#saturation).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Vectorscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vectorscope)

### Vignette

A vignette is a soft dark ring around the edges. It makes the subject in the middle stand out.

**Example.** A portrait where the corners fade to black.

**See also:** [Look](#look).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Vignetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vignetting)

### Waveform monitor

A waveform monitor plots brightness across the picture. Editors use it to keep white from burning and black from turning into mud.

**Example.** Checking that the sky does not go above the top line.

**Also called:** scope. **See also:** [Histogram](#histogram), [Vectorscope](#vectorscope).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Waveform monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveform_monitor)

### White balance

Light has a colour: candles are orange, shade is blue. White balance tells the camera what white looks like in that light so the other colours come out right.

**Example.** In a room lit by warm bulbs, a white wall looks orange until the white balance is set.

**See also:** [Color temperature](#color-temperature), [Tint](#tint).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: White balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_balance)
