Terms on this page: 29. A video is only done when someone can watch it. These words are about exporting it and sending it where it belongs.

### Adaptive streaming

The video is stored in several qualities, and the player switches between them as the internet speed changes.

**Example.** A video becomes blurry for a moment when you walk away from the Wi-Fi.

**Also called:** HLS, DASH. **See also:** [Streaming](#streaming), [Bitrate](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#bitrate).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Adaptive bitrate streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_bitrate_streaming)

### Autoplay

On feeds, videos play by themselves and muted. That is why captions matter: most people watch before they turn the sound on.

**Example.** A video in the feed that starts silently as you scroll.

**See also:** [Caption](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#caption), [Hook](#hook).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Autoplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoplay)

### Backup

Files can be lost. A backup lets you recover. Keep your originals and your project copied in two places.

**Example.** Saving your project to an external drive as well.

**In Stuarty.** See [Backups and upgrades](/docs/self-hosting/backups-and-upgrades).

**See also:** [Project bundle](#project-bundle).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Backup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backup)

### Call to action

A call to action (CTA) tells viewers what to do next: visit, buy, follow, share.

**Example.** "Follow for more tips" at the end of a video.

**Also called:** CTA. **See also:** [Hook](#hook), [Intro and outro](#intro-and-outro).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Call to action (marketing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_to_action_(marketing))

### Commercial

A commercial (or ad) shows a product, says why it is good and ends with how to get it.

**Example.** A 30-second advert for a toy.

**In Stuarty.** Product commercials are one of the two use cases Stuarty is built for.

**Also called:** ad, spot. **See also:** [Call to action](#call-to-action), [Teaser](#teaser).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Television advertisement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_advertisement)

### Deliverable

Different places want different files: one for the web, one for TV, one with captions. Each of them is a deliverable.

**Example.** A square video for a social post and a wide video for a website.

**See also:** [Export](#export), [Master file](#master-file).

### Embed

Embedding puts a video player into a web page, while the file stays on the first site.

**Example.** A YouTube player inside a blog post.

**See also:** [Streaming](#streaming).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Web widget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_widget)

### Export

Exporting builds every frame and sound of the project and saves them as a normal video file you can share. Your project stays unchanged.

**Example.** After editing, you press export and get birthday.mp4.

**In Stuarty.** An export is a job: it renders the current revision, encodes the file and checks it. See [Exporting](/docs/guide/exporting).

**Also called:** render out. **See also:** [Render](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#render), [Deliverable](#deliverable).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Export preset

A preset remembers the format, size and quality for a purpose, like "web" or "social".

**Example.** One click to export with the same settings as last time.

**In Stuarty.** See [Encoders and presets](/docs/guide/encoders-and-presets).

**See also:** [Export](#export), [Bitrate](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#bitrate).

### Frame size limit

Apps limit the size and length of a video. Larger ones are refused or shrunk.

**Example.** A short video app that only takes up to 1080 by 1920.

**In Stuarty.** Stuarty accepts frames up to 4096 pixels on a side.

**See also:** [Resolution](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#resolution), [Platform](#platform).

### Hook

On a feed people decide in a moment. A hook is a question, a surprise or a strong picture right at the start.

**Example.** "You are peeling bananas wrong" in the first second.

**See also:** [Call to action](#call-to-action), [Pacing](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#pacing).

### Intro and outro

An intro (opening) says what the video is or who made it. An outro (ending) wraps it up, often with thanks and a call to action.

**Example.** A channel logo animation at the start and a "thank you" card at the end.

**See also:** [Title](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#title), [Credits](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#credits).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Introduction (writing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_(writing))

### Job

Exporting can take a while. It runs as a job, so you can keep working and check how it is going.

**Example.** A progress bar filling while you do something else.

**In Stuarty.** Exports and transcriptions are jobs. See [Jobs](/docs/guide/jobs).

**See also:** [Export](#export), [Render](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#render).

### Lint

A linter reads your project and warns about problems: text outside the safe area, an empty gap, a clip missing its media.

**Example.** A warning that a caption is too close to the edge.

**In Stuarty.** `stuarty lint` and the `lint` tool. An export is refused while the lint finds an error.

**See also:** [Safe area](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#safe-area), [Export](#export).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Lint (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lint_(software))

### Master file

A master is exported at high quality once. Smaller copies for the web or phones are made from it, so you do not have to edit again.

**Example.** You keep the big master and make a small version for messages.

**See also:** [Deliverable](#deliverable), [Intermediate codec](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#intermediate-codec).

### Platform

Each platform prefers a shape, a size and a length, and it recompresses your video. Knowing the platform helps to choose the settings.

**Example.** A vertical video for TikTok and a wide one for YouTube.

**See also:** [Aspect ratio](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#aspect-ratio), [Re-encoding](#re-encoding).

**Learn more:** [YouTube Help: recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171)

### Poster frame

A poster frame is the picture a player shows until you press play. It can be any frame of the video or a separate image.

**Example.** A still of the main character shown on a web page.

**In Stuarty.** `--format png --at 2s` exports a frame to use as a poster.

**See also:** [Thumbnail](#thumbnail).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Thumbnail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thumbnail)

### Project bundle

A bundle packs the project, its media and fonts together, so someone else can open it on another computer.

**Example.** Sending the whole project to a friend in one file.

**In Stuarty.** `bundle:export` writes a `.vvbundle`. See [Exporting](/docs/guide/exporting).

**Also called:** archive, package. **See also:** [Backup](#backup), [Project](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#project).

### Proxy

Big 4K videos can be slow to play while you edit. A proxy is a small stand-in that is easy to play. The final export still uses the original.

**Example.** Editing with low-resolution copies and exporting with the full ones.

**In Stuarty.** The preview plays a proxy that ingest writes for each video. See [Media library](/docs/guide/media-library).

**Also called:** optimized media. **See also:** [Preview](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#preview), [Intermediate codec](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#intermediate-codec).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Offline editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offline_editing)

### Re-encoding

Every time lossy video is saved again, a bit of quality is lost. Many sites re-encode what you upload, so give them a good source.

**Example.** A video uploaded to a platform looks a little softer than your original.

**See also:** [Lossy and lossless](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#lossy-and-lossless), [Master file](#master-file).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Generation loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_loss) · [YouTube Help: recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171)

### Review

A review is when people watch an unfinished video and say what to change, before the final one.

**Example.** Your teacher watches the draft and asks to shorten the intro.

**Also called:** feedback round, approval. **See also:** [Rough cut](/docs/glossary/industry-slang#rough-cut), [Final cut](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#final-cut).

### Short-form video

Short-form videos are quick, vertical and usually have captions. Examples are Reels, Shorts and TikToks.

**Example.** A 20-second recipe.

**In Stuarty.** Stuarty starts with 9:16 and word-timed captions for this use.

**Also called:** shorts, reels. **See also:** [Vertical video](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#vertical-video), [Caption](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#caption).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Short-form content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-form_content)

### Streaming

A streaming site sends small pieces of video just before you need them. It may lower the quality if your connection is slow.

**Example.** Watching a film that begins at once.

**See also:** [Adaptive streaming](#adaptive-streaming), [Bitrate](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#bitrate).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Streaming media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media)

### Teaser

A teaser shows just a little of a product or a story to get attention before the main release.

**Example.** A 15-second preview of a new game.

**In Stuarty.** See the [Product teaser template](/docs/guide/template-product-teaser).

**See also:** [Trailer](#trailer), [Template](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#template).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Teaser campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaser_campaign)

### Thumbnail

A thumbnail is the cover of a video. A clear, bright one makes more people press play.

**Example.** The picture you see before you click a video.

**See also:** [Poster frame](#poster-frame).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Thumbnail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thumbnail)

### Trailer

A trailer shows the best moments of a movie to make people want to watch it.

**Example.** The two-minute video before a film comes out.

**See also:** [Teaser](#teaser).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film trailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_trailer)

### Verification

After exporting, a program can open the file and check that it has video and sound, the right length and size.

**Example.** Opening a freshly baked cake to make sure it is cooked.

**In Stuarty.** An MP4 export reports success only after both streams and the duration are checked.

**See also:** [Export](#export), [Stream](/docs/glossary/formats-codecs-containers#stream).

### Version

Keeping versions lets you go back to an earlier idea if the new one is worse.

**Example.** "teaser_v3.mp4" is the third try.

**In Stuarty.** Every change raises the project's revision, so you can undo.

**See also:** [Revision](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#revision), [Undo and redo](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#undo-and-redo).

### Watermark

A watermark is a small semi-transparent mark, often in a corner. It helps to say who made the video.

**Example.** A channel logo in the lower right corner.

**See also:** [Overlay](/docs/glossary/effects-and-compositing#overlay), [Opacity](/docs/glossary/effects-and-compositing#opacity).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Digital watermarking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_watermarking)
