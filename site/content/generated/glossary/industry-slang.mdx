Terms on this page: 38. Every job has its own way of talking. This is how video teams talk, with what each word really means.

### A-roll

A-roll is the main story: the interview, the host, the action. The extra pictures that cover it are B-roll.

**Example.** A person telling their story on camera.

**See also:** [B-roll](#b-roll), [Cutaway](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#cutaway).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: B-roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-roll)

### B-roll

While someone talks about their bakery, you show the oven, the dough and the shop sign. Those pictures are B-roll. They add interest and hide cuts.

**Example.** Hands kneading dough while the baker's voice is heard.

**See also:** [A-roll](#a-roll), [Cutaway](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#cutaway).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: B-roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-roll)

### Blocking

Blocking is working out the movements of actors and camera for a scene, before filming it.

**Example.** Marking on the floor with tape where each actor stops.

**See also:** [Coverage](#coverage), [Camera angle](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#camera-angle).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Blocking (stage)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocking_(stage))

### Boom operator

The boom operator keeps the microphone close to people but out of the picture.

**Example.** Someone holding a furry microphone just above the actors' heads.

**Also called:** boom mic. **See also:** [Dialogue](/docs/glossary/audio#dialogue), [Room tone](/docs/glossary/audio#room-tone).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Boom operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_operator)

### Bumper

A bumper is a few seconds that marks the break between segments.

**Example.** A two-second logo animation before the ads.

**Also called:** sting, stinger. **See also:** [Intro and outro](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#intro-and-outro), [Transition](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#transition).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Bumper (broadcasting)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumper_(broadcasting))

### Call sheet

A call sheet lists the time, the place, the scenes and who is needed for a filming day.

**Example.** "Be at the park at 7:00 for scenes 4 and 5."

**See also:** [Shot list](#shot-list), [Production](#production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Call sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_sheet)

### Chyron

"Chyron" is the name TV people use for graphics such as names and headlines.

**Example.** The scrolling headline at the bottom of a news channel.

**Also called:** lower third, CG. **See also:** [Lower third](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#lower-third), [Title](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#title).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Chyron Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chyron_Corporation)

### Cold open

A cold open puts you in the middle of something exciting, and the opening titles come after.

**Example.** A funny moment first, then the show's title.

**See also:** [Hook](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#hook).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cold open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_open)

### Coverage

Good coverage means a wide shot, a medium and some close-ups of the same action. The editor can then choose the best moments and keep the pace.

**Example.** Filming the same conversation from both sides.

**See also:** [Shot list](#shot-list), [Master shot](#master-shot).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Glossary of motion picture terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motion_picture_terms)

### Cutting room floor

"It ended up on the cutting room floor" means it was filmed but not used in the final video.

**Example.** A funny scene removed because the video was too long.

**See also:** [Final cut](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#final-cut), [Trim](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#trim).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cutting room floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_room_floor)

### Dailies

Dailies (or rushes) let the director and editors check what was filmed, so mistakes are caught early.

**Example.** Everyone watches yesterday's clips at lunch.

**Also called:** rushes. **See also:** [Raw footage](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#raw-footage), [Take](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#take).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Dailies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dailies)

### Director

The director leads the team, chooses how each shot looks and works with actors.

**Example.** The person who says "Action!" and "Cut!".

**See also:** [Producer](#producer), [Director of photography](#director-of-photography).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director)

### Director of photography

The DP (or cinematographer) chooses the camera, the lenses and the lighting to create the look.

**Example.** The person who makes the sunset scene look golden.

**Also called:** DP, DoP, cinematographer. **See also:** [Director](#director), [Camera angle](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#camera-angle).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cinematographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematographer)

### Fine cut

The fine cut has tidy cuts and pace. Only small changes and finishing touches remain.

**Example.** The version you show to the teacher for the last comments.

**See also:** [Rough cut](#rough-cut), [Picture lock](#picture-lock).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Fix it in post

Sometimes small problems can be repaired when editing, but it is always better to get it right when filming.

**Example.** A boom microphone appears in a corner; "we will fix it in post".

**See also:** [Post-production](#post-production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Post-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-production)

### Gaffer

The gaffer plans and sets up the lights, working with the director of photography.

**Example.** The person who places a big lamp to make moonlight.

**See also:** [Grip](#grip), [Director of photography](#director-of-photography).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Gaffer (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaffer_(filmmaking))

### Grip

Grips build rigs, dolly tracks and stands for lights and cameras.

**Example.** The people who push the camera on rails.

**See also:** [Gaffer](#gaffer).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Grip (occupation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grip_(occupation))

### Hero shot

A hero shot shows the product or the person at their best, the shot you would put on a poster.

**Example.** The sneaker shining on a pedestal.

**Also called:** beauty shot. **See also:** [Close-up](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#close-up), [Commercial](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#commercial).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Glossary of motion picture terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motion_picture_terms)

### Hit the beat

When something "hits" the beat, it lands at the same moment as the drum. It makes a video feel tight.

**Example.** Each title appears exactly when the snare plays.

**In Vukem Video.** `audio.mark_beats` writes markers on the beats.

**See also:** [Beat](/docs/glossary/audio#beat), [Rhythm](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#rhythm).

### In the can

The old film was kept in cans. "In the can" means the scene is recorded and safe.

**Example.** "That scene is in the can."

**See also:** [That's a wrap](#thats-a-wrap).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Glossary of motion picture terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motion_picture_terms)

### Kill your darlings

Editors use it too: a beautiful shot that slows down the story has to go.

**Example.** Removing your favourite shot because the video is better without it.

**See also:** [Pacing](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#pacing), [Cutting room floor](#cutting-room-floor).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Murder your darlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_your_darlings)

### Master shot

The master shot shows everything that happens. Other angles are cut into it.

**Example.** A wide shot of a whole dinner table scene.

**See also:** [Coverage](#coverage), [Wide shot](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#wide-shot).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Master shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_shot)

### Mood board

A mood board gathers colours, photos and examples to tell a team what feeling the video should have.

**Example.** A board with photos of golden sunsets for a warm summer video.

**See also:** [Look](/docs/glossary/color#look), [Pre-production](#pre-production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Mood board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_board)

### Pickup shot

Sometimes the editor notices something is missing. A pickup shot is a small extra shot made afterwards.

**Example.** A close-up of the key in the lock, filmed a week later.

**Also called:** reshoot, pickups. **See also:** [Coverage](#coverage), [Insert shot](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#insert-shot).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Reshoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reshoot)

### Picture lock

After picture lock, the sound, music and colour teams do their work on a fixed timeline. A change to the picture would break their work.

**Example.** "We have picture lock, send it to the sound mix."

**Also called:** locked picture. **See also:** [Fine cut](#fine-cut), [Post-production](#post-production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Post-production

Post (for short) is the part of making a video that happens once the shooting is done.

**Example.** After filming the trip, you spend a week in post.

**Also called:** post. **See also:** [Pre-production](#pre-production), [Production](#production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Post-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-production)

### Pre-production

Pre-production is everything you decide before the camera rolls, so the shooting is fast and calm.

**Example.** Writing what you will say and choosing where to film.

**Also called:** pre-pro. **See also:** [Production](#production), [Storyboard](#storyboard).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Pre-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-production)

### Producer

A producer makes it possible for the film to happen: finds money, hires people, solves problems.

**Example.** The person who books the location and pays for the equipment.

**See also:** [Director](#director).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_producer)

### Production

Production is the shooting: cameras, actors, sound, lights.

**Example.** The weekend you film the whole short movie.

**See also:** [Pre-production](#pre-production), [Post-production](#post-production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_production)

### Punch in

A punch-in enlarges part of the frame, so the same shot gets a new feel and can be used as a second angle.

**Example.** Zooming 120% on the speaker's face for emphasis.

**In Vukem Video.** Scale an item with `item.set_transform`.

**See also:** [Close-up](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#close-up), [Jump cut](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#jump-cut).

### Rough cut

In a rough cut the story is there, but the timing, sound and colour are not finished.

**Example.** A first assembly of all the best moments of the trip, in order.

**Also called:** assembly. **See also:** [Fine cut](#fine-cut), [Final cut](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#final-cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Rough cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_cut)

### Runner

A runner (or production assistant) fetches things, carries equipment and passes messages. Many careers start here.

**Example.** The person who brings coffee and sets up chairs.

**Also called:** PA, production assistant. **See also:** [Production](#production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Production assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_assistant)

### Script

A script has the words people say and a description of what we see. Even a 30-second video can have one.

**Example.** Three lines of narration for a product video.

**Also called:** screenplay. **See also:** [Storyboard](#storyboard), [Voice-over](/docs/glossary/audio#voice-over).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screenplay)

### Shot list

A shot list lists each shot, so you do not forget one before leaving the place.

**Example.** "Wide of the door, close-up of the hand, cutaway of the clock."

**See also:** [Storyboard](#storyboard), [Coverage](#coverage).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Glossary of motion picture terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motion_picture_terms)

### Showreel

Editors, actors and designers make showreels to show what they can do.

**Example.** A one-minute compilation of your best edits.

**Also called:** demo reel. **See also:** [Sizzle reel](#sizzle-reel).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Showreel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showreel)

### Sizzle reel

A sizzle reel is a pitch video: it shows the most attractive moments in a very short time.

**Example.** A one-minute highlight of a band to convince a venue to book it.

**See also:** [Trailer](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#trailer), [Showreel](#showreel).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sizzle reel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizzle_reel)

### Storyboard

A storyboard looks like a comic strip: one box for each shot, with a note about what happens.

**Example.** A sketch of a close-up of the cake, then a wide of the party.

**See also:** [Shot list](#shot-list), [Pre-production](#pre-production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Storyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storyboard)

### That's a wrap

"Wrap" means the shooting of a scene, a day or the whole film is done.

**Example.** "That's a wrap, thank you everybody!"

**Also called:** wrap. **See also:** [Production](#production).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Glossary of motion picture terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_motion_picture_terms)
