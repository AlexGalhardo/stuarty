Terms on this page: 39. These are the parts of the editing screen and the things you do there every day.

### Asset

An asset is any file the project can use. It sits in the library until you put it on the timeline.

**Example.** A song, a photo and three videos are five assets.

**In Stuarty.** Imported files become assets in the [media library](/docs/guide/media-library).

**Also called:** media. **See also:** [Media library](#media-library), [Clip](#clip).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Batch

A batch groups several small changes. Either all of them happen, or none does, so the project is never half-changed.

**Example.** Add a track, put a clip on it and name it, all in one go.

**In Stuarty.** An `edit` call is a batch. See [Batches, revisions and idempotency](/docs/agents/batches).

**See also:** [Revision](#revision).

### Brand kit

A brand kit keeps the look of a person or a company. Every new video can start with the same colours and fonts.

**Example.** A shop always uses the same orange and the same font in its videos.

**In Stuarty.** See [Brand kit](/docs/guide/brand-kit).

**See also:** [Template](#template), [Font](/docs/glossary/text-and-captions#font).

### Clip

A clip is a piece of an asset on the timeline. You can make it shorter, move it or split it, and the original file stays the same.

**Example.** From a 5-minute asset you use only 10 seconds as a clip.

**In Stuarty.** `clip.add` puts one on the timeline.

**See also:** [Asset](#asset), [Item](#item), [Trim](#trim).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Editor

An editor is a person who cuts and arranges video. People also call the program an editor.

**Example.** "I am the editor of our school video" means you decide the cuts.

**In Stuarty.** Stuarty is an editor that both people and AI agents can use.

**See also:** [Video editing](#video-editing).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editor)

### Frame accuracy

A frame-accurate editor shows the same frame every time you stop on it. That is how you can cut exactly when the ball touches the bat.

**Example.** Cutting on the exact frame where a door shuts.

**In Stuarty.** The paused preview is the frame that the export produces.

**See also:** [Frame](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#frame), [Timecode](#timecode).

### Gap

A gap shows black (or the layer below) when played. Editors usually close gaps, so the video does not go blank.

**Example.** You delete a clip and a hole is left, a gap, until you close it.

**In Stuarty.** `timeline.close_gap`.

**See also:** [Ripple](#ripple).

### In point and out point

Set an in point where you want the clip to begin and an out point where it ends. Everything outside is left unused.

**Example.** From a long clip of a swing, you mark the 3 seconds where the kid jumps off.

**Also called:** mark in, mark out. **See also:** [Trim](#trim), [Clip](#clip).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Insert edit

An insert makes room: the new clip goes in, and what came after moves to the right.

**Example.** Insert a close-up between two shots; the rest of the video becomes longer.

**See also:** [Overwrite edit](#overwrite-edit), [Ripple](#ripple).

### Item

In Stuarty, item is the general word for everything you place on a track, whatever its kind.

**Example.** A video clip, a title and a rectangle behind the title are three items.

**In Stuarty.** Every operation names an `item_id`. See [Tracks, clips and items](/docs/concepts/tracks-clips-items).

**See also:** [Clip](#clip), [Track](#track).

### Layer

Layers are stacked. What is on top hides what is below, unless the top one is see-through.

**Example.** A drawing of a hat placed over a photo of your face is a layer on top of another.

**See also:** [Track](#track), [Opacity](/docs/glossary/effects-and-compositing#opacity), [Compositing](/docs/glossary/effects-and-compositing#compositing).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Layers (digital image editing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layers_(digital_image_editing))

### Marker

A marker is a small flag with an optional name. It does not change the video. It just helps you find a place again.

**Example.** A marker on every beat of a song, to line up the cuts.

**In Stuarty.** `marker.add`, `marker.set` and `marker.remove`; `M` in the editor. See [Basic timeline editing](/docs/guide/timeline).

**See also:** [Beat](/docs/glossary/audio#beat), [Timeline](#timeline).

### Media library

The media library lists everything you imported. You pick things from it and drop them on the timeline.

**Example.** Like a toy box: everything is inside, and you take out what you need.

**Also called:** bin, media pool. **See also:** [Asset](#asset), [Timeline](#timeline).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Metadata

Metadata is data about data: the date, the camera, the length of a file. It is not part of the picture.

**Example.** A phone video file knows the time it was recorded.

**In Stuarty.** `media_inspect` shows an asset's metadata. Text taken from a file is treated as untrusted. See [Safety](/docs/agents/safety).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Metadata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metadata)

### Multicam

When two or three cameras film one event, a multicam edit lets you switch between them, like a TV director.

**Example.** A concert filmed from the front and from the side: you pick the best view moment by moment.

**See also:** [Cut](/docs/glossary/cuts-and-transitions#cut), [Camera angle](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#camera-angle).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Multiple-camera setup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple-camera_setup)

### Nesting

A nest (or compound clip) is a clip that has its own little timeline inside. You can move or change the whole group at once.

**Example.** Group a photo, a title and music into one block and move the block.

**Also called:** compound clip. **See also:** [Sequence](#sequence), [Clip](#clip).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Overwrite edit

An overwrite covers the old part of the timeline with the new clip. The total length does not change.

**Example.** You overwrite two seconds of the video with a funnier clip.

**See also:** [Insert edit](#insert-edit).

### Playhead

The playhead is a thin line that moves while the video plays. You can drag it to jump to any moment.

**Example.** Put the playhead where the cake appears, then press split.

**Also called:** current-time indicator, CTI. **See also:** [Scrubbing](#scrubbing), [Timecode](#timecode).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Preview

The preview window plays what you have built so far. It may be a little rougher than the final file so it can play fast.

**Example.** You press play to check that the title appears at the right time.

**In Stuarty.** See [Preview and playback](/docs/guide/preview).

**See also:** [Render](#render), [Proxy](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#proxy).

### Project

A project keeps your list of clips, where you put them and every change. Your original videos are not changed: the project only points at them.

**Example.** Closing the program and opening it tomorrow, your video is exactly as you left it.

**In Stuarty.** A [project](/docs/projects) is one JSON document; every change is a batch of operations that raises its revision.

**See also:** [Timeline](#timeline), [Asset](#asset).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Render

Rendering is when the computer works out every frame, with all the layers, colours and effects added, and writes them to a file.

**Example.** After you press export, a bar fills while the computer renders your video.

**In Stuarty.** See [Render: one plan, two executors](/docs/concepts/render).

**See also:** [Export](/docs/glossary/delivery-and-platforms#export), [Preview](#preview).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Rendering (computer graphics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendering_(computer_graphics))

### Revision

Think of saving a game after every move. The revision is the move number. It lets two helpers know whether the project changed while they were thinking.

**Example.** You and an agent both look at revision 12. You change it, so it is 13. The agent has to look again before changing anything.

**In Stuarty.** See [Commands, batches and revisions](/docs/concepts/commands-and-revisions).

**See also:** [Batch](#batch), [Undo and redo](#undo-and-redo).

### Ripple

In a ripple edit, if you make a clip shorter, all the clips after it slide to the left to fill the hole. Like pulling a card out of a row and the others close up.

**Example.** Ripple delete a clip and the next clips move closer by themselves.

**In Stuarty.** `ripple` on `item.trim` and `item.delete`; `Shift+Delete`.

**See also:** [Gap](#gap), [Roll](#roll), [Slide](#slide).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Roll

A roll edit moves the point where one clip ends and the next begins. One clip gets longer and its neighbour gets shorter by the same amount.

**Example.** You want the next shot to appear half a second sooner: roll the cut left.

**In Stuarty.** `roll: true` on `item.trim`.

**Also called:** rolling edit. **See also:** [Ripple](#ripple), [Slide](#slide), [Slip](#slip).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Scrubbing

When you scrub, you slide through the video with your mouse, like turning the pages of a book quickly with your thumb.

**Example.** Scrubbing to find the moment the goal happened.

**See also:** [Playhead](#playhead).

### Sequence

In some programs a project can hold many timelines, and each one is a sequence. The word also means a run of shots that tell one part of the story.

**Example.** You make one sequence for the trailer and another for the whole video.

**See also:** [Timeline](#timeline), [Scene](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#scene).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Sequence (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_(filmmaking))

### Slide

In a slide edit the clip keeps showing the same piece of video, but starts and ends at a different time. The clips next to it get longer and shorter.

**Example.** Slide a short clip a bit to the right so it covers a different moment of the talk.

**In Stuarty.** `slide: true` on `item.move`.

**See also:** [Roll](#roll), [Slip](#slip).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Slip

In a slip edit the clip stays in the same place on the timeline, with the same length, but shows a different part of the original video.

**Example.** A 3-second clip of a jump: slip it to show the moment a little later.

**In Stuarty.** `item.slip`; `Alt+,` and `Alt+.` in the editor.

**See also:** [Slide](#slide), [Roll](#roll), [Trim](#trim).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Slot

A slot says: put a picture here, or a name here. Fill every slot and the template becomes your own video.

**Example.** A product teaser template has slots for the product photo and the price.

**In Stuarty.** `slot.mark`, `slot.fill`. See [Templates](/docs/guide/templates).

**See also:** [Template](#template).

### Snapping

With snapping on, a clip you drag jumps neatly against the nearby edge, like a magnet, so there is no tiny gap.

**Example.** Drag a title toward the end of a clip and it clicks right at the edge.

**In Stuarty.** `N` turns snapping on and off.

**See also:** [Gap](#gap), [Marker](#marker).

### Source and program monitor

Many editors have a source window to look at a raw clip and choose the part to use, and a program window to watch the edit.

**Example.** Look at the full clip in the source monitor, mark the best five seconds, then add them to the timeline.

**Also called:** source monitor, program monitor. **See also:** [In point and out point](#in-point-and-out-point), [Preview](#preview).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Split

Splitting puts a cut in the middle of a clip. You get two clips side by side, and you can delete or move either.

**Example.** Split a long clip where the boring part starts, then delete the boring part.

**In Stuarty.** `item.split`, and `Mod+B` in the editor.

**Also called:** blade, razor, cut. **See also:** [Trim](#trim).

### Template

A template is a finished design where you only swap the picture, the words or the colours, like a form you fill in.

**Example.** A quote card where you change only the quote and the author.

**In Stuarty.** See [Templates](/docs/guide/templates).

**See also:** [Slot](#slot), [Brand kit](#brand-kit).

### Timecode

Timecode gives every frame an exact address, like 00:01:30:12. It is how editors talk about an exact moment.

**Example.** "Cut at one minute thirty" is 00:01:30:00.

**In Stuarty.** Stuarty writes times like `2s` or `97f`. See [Time](/docs/concepts/time).

**See also:** [Frame](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#frame), [Frame rate](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#frame-rate).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: SMPTE timecode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMPTE_timecode) · [SMPTE, the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers](https://www.smpte.org/)

### Timeline

The timeline is a long ruler of time. Left is the start of the video, right is the end. Clips sit on it in the order they are played.

**Example.** Drag a clip to the right and it plays later in the video.

**In Stuarty.** See [Basic timeline editing](/docs/guide/timeline).

**See also:** [Track](#track), [Clip](#clip), [Playhead](#playhead).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Track

Tracks are rows. A clip on a higher video track is drawn on top of the clips below it. Sound goes on audio tracks.

**Example.** Put a title on the track above your video so the title shows on top of it.

**In Stuarty.** A track is either visual or audio. See [Tracks, clips and items](/docs/concepts/tracks-clips-items).

**Also called:** layer, channel. **See also:** [Layer](#layer), [Clip](#clip), [Timeline](#timeline).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Video editing software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_editing_software)

### Trim

Trimming cuts off the beginning or the end of a clip. You are not deleting the video file, only choosing which part you show.

**Example.** Trim the first two seconds of a clip where nothing happens yet.

**In Stuarty.** `item.trim`. See [Trim, split and move clips](/docs/guide/editing-tools).

**See also:** [Split](#split), [In point and out point](#in-point-and-out-point).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)

### Undo and redo

Mistakes are cheap in a video editor. Undo steps back, redo steps forward again.

**Example.** You delete the wrong clip, press undo, and it is back.

**In Stuarty.** Each person or agent has their own undo history. See [Undo and history](/docs/guide/undo-and-history).

**See also:** [Revision](#revision).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Undo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undo)

### Video editing

Editing is deciding what stays, what goes and in what order, then adding sound, words and effects. It is where a pile of clips becomes a video.

**Example.** From ten minutes of a birthday party you keep a one-minute video of the best moments.

**See also:** [Footage](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#footage), [Rough cut](/docs/glossary/industry-slang#rough-cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_editing)
