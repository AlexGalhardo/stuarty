Terms on this page: 29. A video is a chain of shots. These words describe how one shot turns into the next.

### 180-degree rule

Imagine a line between two people. If you keep every camera on one side of it, the first person always stays on the left. Cross the line and they seem to swap places.

**Example.** In a car chase the red car stays on the left of the screen in every shot.

**See also:** [Continuity](#continuity), [Shot and reverse shot](#shot-and-reverse-shot).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: 180-degree rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180-degree_rule)

### Continuity

Continuity means no tiny mistakes between shots: the same glass is full, the same shirt is worn. Viewers notice when something changes by itself.

**Example.** A boy is wearing a hat in one shot and not in the next, with no reason.

**See also:** [Cut on action](#cut-on-action), [180-degree rule](#180-degree-rule).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Continuity (fiction)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_(fiction)) · [Wikipedia: Continuity editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_editing)

### Cross-cutting

Cross-cutting shows two actions in turns so the viewer feels they happen together, which builds excitement.

**Example.** A race to catch a bus, cut with the bus driver closing the doors.

**Also called:** parallel editing. **See also:** [Montage](#montage).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cross-cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cutting)

### Crossfade

A crossfade overlaps two clips and swaps their strength. It is a dissolve for pictures and a smooth blend for sounds.

**Example.** Two songs overlap for three seconds so the music never stops.

**In Stuarty.** `item.crossfade` sets matching fades on two overlapping items of different tracks.

**See also:** [Dissolve](#dissolve), [Fade](#fade).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Crossfade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossfade)

### Cut

A cut is the simplest change: one picture is replaced by another in a single frame. Most of a video is held together by cuts.

**Example.** You see a boy kick a ball, then cut to the goalkeeper's face.

**Also called:** hard cut, straight cut. **See also:** [Transition](#transition), [Jump cut](#jump-cut), [Cut on action](#cut-on-action).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cut (transition)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_(transition))

### Cut on action

If a person starts to stand up in one shot and you cut during the movement to another angle, the viewer's eye follows the motion and does not notice the cut.

**Example.** A kid starts to throw the ball in a wide shot, and the cut lands in the middle of the throw in a close shot.

**Also called:** match on action. **See also:** [Cut](#cut), [Continuity](#continuity).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Continuity editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_editing)

### Cutaway

A cutaway leaves the main shot to show something related, then comes back. Editors also use it to hide a jump cut.

**Example.** Someone talks about a trip, and you cut away to the photo of the beach.

**See also:** [B-roll](/docs/glossary/industry-slang#b-roll), [Jump cut](#jump-cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cutaway (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutaway_(filmmaking))

### Dip to black

The screen goes fully black for a moment before the next scene appears. It feels like blinking, or a chapter ending.

**Example.** The end of a school day dips to black, then the next morning begins.

**Also called:** dip to white. **See also:** [Fade](#fade), [Dissolve](#dissolve).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Fade (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fade_(filmmaking))

### Dissolve

In a dissolve, the first shot becomes see-through while the next one becomes solid, so for a moment you see both mixed.

**Example.** A summer beach slowly melts into a snowy mountain to show time passing.

**Also called:** cross dissolve. **See also:** [Crossfade](#crossfade), [Fade](#fade).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Dissolve (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolve_(filmmaking))

### Easing

Easing changes the speed of a change, so it begins slowly, speeds up, then slows to a stop. It looks natural, like a car stopping smoothly.

**Example.** A title slides in fast and softly settles in its place.

**In Stuarty.** Keyframes can have an easing. See [Animate with keyframes](/docs/guide/keyframes).

**Also called:** ease in, ease out. **See also:** [Keyframe](/docs/glossary/effects-and-compositing#keyframe).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Inbetweening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbetweening)

### Fade

A fade in starts from black and the picture slowly appears. A fade out slowly goes dark. Sound can fade the same way.

**Example.** The movie ends and the screen slowly goes to black.

**In Stuarty.** A `fade` transition, or the fade in and out of an audio item. See [Transitions](/docs/guide/transitions).

**Also called:** fade in, fade out, fade to black. **See also:** [Dissolve](#dissolve), [Dip to black](#dip-to-black).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Fade (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fade_(filmmaking))

### Final cut

The final cut is the version everyone agreed on. After it, only small fixes (like sound and colour) are made.

**Example.** The version of your video that you upload.

**See also:** [Rough cut](/docs/glossary/industry-slang#rough-cut), [Picture lock](/docs/glossary/industry-slang#picture-lock).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Cut (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_(filmmaking))

### Freeze frame

A freeze frame holds one frame still. It is a good way to end on a funny face or to make a point.

**Example.** The jump freezes in mid-air and the title appears.

**See also:** [Frame](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#frame), [Slow motion](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#slow-motion).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Freeze frame shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeze_frame_shot)

### Handles

Handles are the part of a clip you did not use but still have. They let you make a transition or move a cut later.

**Example.** You use seconds 5 to 8 of a clip, and the seconds before and after are your handles.

**See also:** [Trim](/docs/glossary/editing-and-timeline#trim), [Transition](#transition).

### Iris

An iris transition closes the picture to a small circle, like an old silent movie. It looks old-fashioned on purpose.

**Example.** A cartoon ends with a circle closing around the hero's face.

**See also:** [Wipe](#wipe).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Iris out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_out)

### J-cut

A J-cut is the opposite of an L-cut: the new sound begins first, then the picture arrives. It makes the story flow.

**Example.** You hear a train whistle while still seeing the kitchen, then you cut to the train.

**See also:** [L-cut](#l-cut), [Cut](#cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Split edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_edit)

### Jump cut

If you cut out a piece of a single shot and join the two halves, the person seems to jump to a new pose. It can be used on purpose to be funny, fast or modern.

**Example.** A vlogger cuts out every pause between sentences, so the video is quick and energetic.

**In Stuarty.** `clip.remove_silences` makes exactly this kind of cut. See [Silence removal](/docs/guide/silence-removal).

**See also:** [Cut](#cut), [Cutaway](#cutaway).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Jump cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jump_cut)

### L-cut

In an L-cut you still hear the first scene while the picture has already moved to the next. On the timeline the pieces make the shape of an L.

**Example.** You see a boy's face, but you still hear his friend finish talking.

**Also called:** J-cut, split edit. **See also:** [J-cut](#j-cut), [Cut](#cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Split edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_edit)

### Match cut

In a match cut, something in the first shot (a shape, a motion) matches something in the second. The viewer sees the link even if the places are far apart.

**Example.** A spinning basketball cuts to a spinning planet.

**See also:** [Cut on action](#cut-on-action).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Match cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_cut)

### Montage

A montage shows many small moments in a row, often with music, to show something that took a long time.

**Example.** Ten seconds of training shots: first jumps, then falls, then a perfect jump.

**See also:** [Cross-cutting](#cross-cutting), [Cut](#cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Montage (filmmaking)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montage_(filmmaking))

### Pacing

Short shots make a video feel quick and busy. Long shots make it calm. A good editor changes the pace to match the story.

**Example.** A chase has fast cuts; a sunset has a long, quiet shot.

**See also:** [Cut](#cut), [Rhythm](#rhythm).

### Reverse

When you reverse a clip, time runs backwards: a splash goes back into the cup. It is a fun trick.

**Example.** Smashing a glass, played backwards: the pieces fly together.

**See also:** [Slow motion](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#slow-motion).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Time reversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_reversal)

### Rhythm

Editors cut to a rhythm. Cutting on the beat of a song makes a video feel tidy and exciting.

**Example.** Each new photo appears exactly on a drum hit.

**In Stuarty.** `audio.mark_beats` puts markers on the beats of a song so you can cut on them.

**See also:** [Beat](/docs/glossary/audio#beat), [Pacing](#pacing).

### Shot and reverse shot

You show one person, then the other, back and forth, so it feels like a conversation.

**Example.** Two friends arguing: a shot of the first, then of the second, and so on.

**Also called:** reverse shot. **See also:** [Over-the-shoulder shot](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#over-the-shoulder-shot), [180-degree rule](#180-degree-rule).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Shot reverse shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_reverse_shot)

### Smash cut

A smash cut jumps at once between two very different moments, to surprise or to make you laugh.

**Example.** Someone says "I will never eat that!" and the next shot, they are eating it.

**See also:** [Cut](#cut), [Montage](#montage).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Smash cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smash_cut)

### Speed ramp

A speed ramp makes a clip speed up or slow down in the middle, like a skateboard that rushes and then floats at the top of a jump.

**Example.** A bike video runs at normal speed, slows when the rider flies, then speeds again.

**In Stuarty.** `clip.set_speed` sets one constant speed from 0.25x to 4x for a clip. A speed that changes during the clip is not available.

**See also:** [Slow motion](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#slow-motion), [Easing](#easing).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Slow motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_motion)

### Transition

A transition is how you get from one clip to another when it is not a plain cut: a fade, a dissolve, a wipe. Use them sparingly: too many look silly.

**Example.** The picture fades to black at the end of a day and fades up on the next morning.

**In Stuarty.** `item.set_transition` sets a fade or a slide on the start or the end of an item. See [Transitions](/docs/guide/transitions).

**See also:** [Fade](#fade), [Dissolve](#dissolve), [Wipe](#wipe).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Film transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_transition)

### Whip pan

A whip pan is a pan so fast that everything is a smear. A cut hidden in the blur makes the jump to the next place feel smooth.

**Example.** The camera whips away from a boy and comes back to the same boy in a new place.

**See also:** [Pan](/docs/glossary/shooting-and-footage#pan), [Cut](#cut).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Whip pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_pan)

### Wipe

In a wipe, the next picture is revealed by a moving edge, like pulling a curtain. Slide and push transitions are cousins of it.

**Example.** A cartoon-style line moves from left to right and shows the next scene.

**In Stuarty.** The `slide_left`, `slide_right`, `slide_up` and `slide_down` transitions move the picture on or off the screen.

**See also:** [Transition](#transition).

**Learn more:** [Wikipedia: Wipe (transition)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipe_(transition))
